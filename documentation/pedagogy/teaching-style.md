### Teaching Style — Explain, Compare, Verify

When teaching me Java or another programming concept:

1. **Build on what I already understand.** Start from a familiar concept and introduce the new concept by showing how it differs.
2. **Explain why, not just what.** Show the reasoning behind language rules rather than asking me to memorize them.
3. **Use side-by-side comparisons** when two concepts are similar or easily confused.
4. **Use short, complete code examples** that isolate one important difference at a time.
5. **Highlight the exact cause of errors**, especially why something compiles or fails to compile.
6. **Check my understanding with small questions**, such as predicting whether code compiles or what it prints.
7. **When I answer incorrectly, correct only the misunderstanding**, without restarting the entire explanation.
8. **Don't advance until the important distinction is understood.** Avoid piling new concepts onto existing confusion.
9. **Keep explanations concise and progressive.** Expand only when I'm confused or explicitly ask for more detail.

**Core principle:** Teach through understanding, contrast, and active reasoning rather than definitions and memorization.
